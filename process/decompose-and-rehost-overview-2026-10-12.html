Decompose and Rehost: two pipelines for Mendix apps that already exist

One toolkit, two new entry modes. Decompose splits a monolith into two to four apps by scoring where the cut is cheap and where the platform forbids it. Rehost reads an existing .mpr and regenerates it as MDL into a fresh Mendix 11 app, with a ledger of what did not survive. Both run headless, with mxcli in a cloud container, no Studio Pro.

Decisions already taken: Mendix 9 and above only, version upgrades are not a track. No Event Broker licence, so Business Events is not an integration lane. Slices land in a fresh Mendix 11 app. The monolith side is always a copy; the downloaded original stays read-only.

Why these two, and why together

A Mendix monolith cannot be split by export and import. Apps share no database, external entities cannot own associations or aggregate, no workflow or security role may span two apps, and every app is its own cloud node and licence. So a split is a sequence of regenerations: each slice is read out of the monolith and rebuilt in a new app, and every edge that crossed the cut becomes an OData entity, a published action, a REST call, or a blocker that has to be redesigned first.

That makes Rehost the engine inside Decompose. Rehost alone answers "can we move this app to Mendix 11 as MDL, and what do we lose", with a number instead of an opinion. Decompose adds the question "where do we cut, and what does each cut cost". The take-apart-or-rebuild decision falls out of the Rehost ledger: if more of a module is non-expressible than expressible, rebuild it; otherwise move it.

How Decompose works

  1. MapExisting instruments: app-facts.sh (module edges per ref kind), app-layer-map.sh (levels, backward edges, tangles), the mxcli catalog. Read-only on the model.
  2. Scope (interview, stop and ask)Why split (team, cadence, licence, risk), how many apps may exist, which lanes are allowed. Answers land in the project's PROJECT.md as CONFIRMED or ASSUMED.
  3. Cut scoringbin/cut-score.sh: for a proposed partition, every crossing edge gets the lane its ref kind must become and a cost class; per partition a verdict clean, costly or blocked, with the denominator printed. The user picks or amends the cut.
  4. Landscape blueprintApps, published services per edge, identity broker (OIDC Provider), data ownership per entity. Reuses architecture-blueprint.md.
  5. Slice loop (strangler)Per slice: regenerate its modules into the new app with the Rehost extractor, replace each crossing edge by a published service on one side and a consumed one on the other, run the regression baseline on both sides, copy owned data, retire the module in the monolith. The monolith stays live throughout.
  6. VerifyOne journey crosses each scored edge at least once. Denominator: all scored edges.

How Rehost works

  1. Read probemxcli -p old.mpr -c "list modules" on the real file. A failure is an mxcli bug report and the track stops.
  2. Extractpipelines/mendix-mpr/rehost-extract.sh: the MDL statement describe module M with all per own module, flows reordered into call order, image collections appended, CRLF inside literals kept byte for byte. Marketplace modules are recorded by version and reinstalled, never regenerated.
  3. Fidelity ledgerOne row per source document: emitted, identical, drift, stub (Java body), not expressible, marketplace, hand-off. Denominator in the header. This is the artifact that sizes the hand-fix work and decides rebuild versus move.
  4. Regeneratemxcli new --version 11.x, reinstall marketplace modules, exec module scripts in dependency order, then the headless gates: mx check and mxbuild.
  5. Verifymxcli diff --format struct old against new per module, target zero drift. The regression baseline from the existing-app-assurance skill replayed on the new app.

The self-improvement loop

The approach improves itself through the toolkit's existing discipline, applied every increment. Nothing is written from imagination: each increment runs on a real target, the verbatim output becomes a fixture, the findings become inbox files, and promotion into skills, bug logs and instruments happens in commits that cite the field run.

What the loop has already produced today

  • CRLF inside MDL string literals is load-bearing. Found on the banking round trip, promoted into learned-mdl-preflight.md the same day.
  • app-facts.sh no longer needs the sqlite3 CLI. Found because the cloud container has none. Fixed, cited, committed.
  • cut-score.sh rendered 10 KB table cells on FSM. Capped, fixture re-run, committed.
  • 14 mxcli findings and MDL learnings filed in contrib/inbox for triage into the bug ledger and upstream reports.
  • Two lane-table gaps that only real data could show. Both documented with the verbatim FSM facts committed as a sample, so the next classifier change has a real fixture.

The rules that make it a loop and not a diary. Golden input is captured, never hand-written. Every completion criterion carries a denominator (155 of 155, 56 of 56, 312 edges). Every instrument commit names the project it ran against. A pass nobody performed reports PENDING, never green by absence. Company-specific material (your architectural-risk rubric, client-shaped learnings) goes to the company brain, never to the public toolkit.

Skills: yes, two, and deliberately not yet

The plan reserves skills/decompose-app.md and skills/rehost-app.md, plus entry-mode rows in the runbook and routing-table lines. They are not written yet, on purpose. The toolkit's authoring rules say a behaviour-shaping skill is written for one trigger, pairs each rule with the concrete failure it prevents, and is baseline-tested like an instrument. We have the instruments and the first real failures. We do not yet have the judgement steps exercised end to end: nobody has rewired a crossing edge or decided a Rehost done-criterion on real output. Increment 3 produces exactly that evidence.

skills/decompose-app.md

Trigger: "split", "decompose", "two apps", "microservices" on an existing Mendix app. Owns the Scope interview, reading a cut score (what blocked means, when to copy a module, when to retire one), the landscape blueprint section, and the slice loop with its per-edge rewiring recipes. Cites cut-score.sh.

skills/rehost-app.md

Trigger: "rebuild", "rehost", "move to Mendix 11", "read this .mpr". Owns what to regenerate and what to hand-fix, reading the fidelity ledger, the done-criterion, marketplace reinstall, and the MPK-versus-MDL choice once the probe reports.

Company brain (private repo)

Your house architectural-risk rubric, the licensing rules you apply, and real decomposition experience. The public skill carries the method; the private one carries the judgement that is yours. Template already exists under templates/company-brain/.
