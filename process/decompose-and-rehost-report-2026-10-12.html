Decompose & Rehost: what we built, what it cost, what holds

For a product head who knows Mendix and did not watch the work. Each section opens with its answer; the detail follows.

The answers

How

Score the cuts, agree a target architecture, move each slice as an MPK package, account for every crossing call in a ledger, link the apps over OData and REST, then delete the old modules. One slice at a time, the monolith stays live.

Route

Move modules as MPK packages (byte-identical, IDs kept). Use MDL only to build the edges and seams. Rehost stays a separate track for re-platforming a whole app.

Proof

Eight increments on one public template. One slice (Assets & Parts) was fully strangled; a second (Dispatch & Workforce) reached build-green with a live seam.

Speed

With the skills as playbook, Dispatch took about 1 h 55 to the level that took about 3 h 10 for Assets & Parts (increments 5 and 6), on a slice with about 1.8× the cross-app calls.

mxcli

47 defects ready to file. A nightly build fixed 15 of 50 retested; microflows can now be written into Mendix 9 projects.

Tokens

7,883,334 across 74 measured workers: Opus 3,244,089, Haiku 3,355,386, Sonnet 1,104,928, Fable 178,931.

1. How the pipeline works

Answer: one capability at a time, in gated steps, with a ledger that accounts for every call crossing the cut. The monolith stays live throughout.

Terms, once. MDL is Mendix's text language for describing a model. mxcli is the command-line tool that reads and writes it. MPK is Mendix's own module package file. A slice is the part of the app we cut out. A seam is the live link between the old app and the slice. A stub is a placeholder that compiles but does nothing yet. To strangle is to delete the old part once the slice has taken over.

MonolithOne app, many modules, tangled calls.
→
Slices, one at a timeMove, link with a seam, prove, then delete the old part.
→
LandscapeSeveral apps, each owning its data, linked by OData and REST.

The Decompose pipeline, step by step

As it stands in skills/decompose-app.md. Every decision lands in PROJECT.md as CONFIRMED or ASSUMED. Gated stages end the turn and wait for the user.

  1. Stage 0: Preconditions and scopeMendix 9 or later. Work on a copy. A 9.x monolith is first upgraded to 10.24 (mx convert), as a means, not a deliverable. Lanes are OData and REST only. Scope questions: why split, how many apps, may a slice leave Mendix, is data migration in scope.
  2. Stage 1: Read and scoreRead the app, score candidate cuts by crossing calls, rank slices on business fit and coupling, not on coupling alone.
  3. Stage 1b: Target architecture (gated)Eight sections before any cut: landscape, module-to-app map (every own module assigned), data ownership, contract per seam, auth, cut order, mobile partition, non-goals.
  4. Stage 2: Sequence the cutsFollow dependency direction. The monolith publishes the services a slice needs before the slice is cut. Check access-rule XPath that cannot cross apps.
  5. Stage 3: Move the sliceCreate the target app, export each module as an MPK, install dependencies first, then the slice.
  6. Stage 4: Account for every edgeThe cut ledger (below).
  7. Stage 5: Build the seamReplace stubs by OData reads and REST writes, with security on and errors handled.
  8. Stage 5b: StrangleLevel 3 only. Re-point the monolith at the slice, then delete the slice modules from the monolith.
  9. Stage 6: Prove and report the levelBuild, check, journeys across both apps. The report names exactly one level.
  10. Stage 7: LearningsFeed what broke back into the skills.

The three done levels

LevelMeans
1 build-greenLedger has 0 unresolved rows; mx check 0 errors; build succeeds; moved documents describe-identical.
2 seam-liveEvery stub that carries behaviour is replaced by a working call, or handed off with a written reason. Security on, roles mapped.
3 strangledMonolith calls into the slice are rewired or handed off, the old modules are deleted, and one journey per crossing kind (read, write, join, sync pull, offline forward) passes with data checked in both databases, twice in a row.

The cut ledger

One row per reference that crosses the cut, in both directions. Each row is resolved (moved, copied, OData, REST), a stub, a hand-off with a named reason, retired, or platform. The script exits non-zero while any row is unresolved. The stub count is the progress measure; a stub never counts as done. At level 3 the monolith's in-edge hand-offs become the measure.

The seam patterns, in plain words

NeedPattern
Identify a record across appsGive each shared entity a stable key (a PublicID auto-number).
Who may callOne machine role per service, nothing else. Credentials live in each app's machine-local constants, never in the model.
ReadsOData, shown in the other app as external entities. One consumer module per service.
Writes with behaviourA published REST action with simple parameters and a text result.
FailureEvery REST call has an error handler. OData-backed pages have no error path, so a service outage is visible.
Joins across appsNo association across apps. Filter on the key, using a copy of the other side's key.
DeletesGo through the owner's own delete flow, so its rules still run.
FormatsEnumerations travel as text; dates as UTC ISO text.
ChangeVersion the path. A changed member is a new contract.
OwnershipOnce the slice owns an entity, a local save beside the REST call is a dual write and stays a hand-off until one side goes.

Limits: external entities cannot own associations, cannot aggregate, cannot sync offline, and cannot carry behaviour. That is why writes use REST and offline data arrives as REST-fed copies.

2. How that raised the Rehost vs MPK question

Answer: moving a slice by MPK beats regenerating it as MDL, so the route is a hybrid: MPK moves the modules, MDL builds the edges and seams.

To move a slice we first planned to regenerate it as MDL (Rehost). The MPK probe then showed that exporting a module as a package is byte-identical and keeps entity and unit IDs across 9.24, 10.24 and 11.13. Export takes under 1 s, install about 8 s per module. Caveat: Mendix refuses a 9.x package into 11, so a 9.x source hops through 10.24 first (about 35 s).

Rehost as its own track

Rehost re-platforms a whole app: describe each module to MDL, check it, write it into a fresh project with a repair loop (check, quarantine, execute, resume), then describe the target and compare. The result is a fidelity ledger. It works on small same-version apps and breaks on limits: Java action type parameters, rules, legacy widgets, native layouts, mutual recursion, and 9.x authoring.

Why MDL is valuable even without a rebuild

Describing an app as text is useful on its own, because reading and comparing do not need the text to be re-executable.

  • Understanding: the whole model is searchable. “Who calls this flow” becomes a search.
  • Review: diff the text before and after a change, like a code review.
  • Regression baseline: describe before and after an upgrade or package move. What drifted is what to test.
  • Test scoping: the call graph shows what a change touches.
  • Audits: scan access rules, constants and anti-patterns as text.

Rule of thumb: describe every app you touch, even when you move it by MPK.

Rehost on GenAI Showcase (Mendix 11.12, 48 modules)

36 modules regenerated; 2,776 catalog documents: 579 identical, 271 drift, 1,993 failed. 29 of 36 modules landed. mx check 14 errors, build failed. 1,520 of the failures were seven modules our own repair step dropped, a toolkit defect, not a Mendix limit.
