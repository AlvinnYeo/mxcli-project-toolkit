Decompose & Rehost: what we did, what it cost, what holds

For a product head who knows Mendix and did not watch the work. Six questions, answered first; the detail follows.

The six answers

1. Approach

Two tracks. Rehost regenerates a whole app as text (MDL) into a fresh project. Decompose splits one app into several, one slice at a time, with the old app staying live.

2. App to MDL

A small same-version app came across almost perfectly (154 of 155 identical). A 48-module app did not: 29 of 36 modules landed and the build failed with 14 errors.

3. Rebuilt differently

Seven plan items changed. The big one: we move modules as packages (MPK) and use MDL only for the edges, because the probe proved IDs survive.

4. Time

Single steps take seconds to a minute. A web slice took about 2 h 10 min end to end; adding core services took about 1 h.

5. Tokens

3,010,761 tokens across 17 measured workers: Opus 1,907,879, Sonnet 923,951, Fable 178,931. The orchestrator itself is not counted.

6. Proven and next

Build-green slices and one live cross-app journey are proven. Strangling the monolith, mobile, login across apps and data migration are not.

1. The approach

Two plain terms first. MDL is Mendix's text language for describing a model; mxcli is the command-line tool that reads and writes it. MPK is Mendix's own module package file. A slice is the part of an app we cut out and move.

TrackWhat it doesDone means
RehostReads an existing app, regenerates every module as MDL, runs it into a fresh Mendix 11 project. Checks and repairs in a loop.Build-green with hand-offs (below)
DecomposeScores where to cut, moves a slice, accounts for every call that crosses the cut, builds the link between the apps.Three levels: build-green, seam-live, strangled

The self-improvement loop

  • Workers drop findings into an inbox. Triage promotes them into the bug ledger or into skills.
  • Every user-visible change adds a CHANGELOG.md line in the same commit, crediting the source project.
  • Skills were written from the field runs, then corrected by their first real use. Example: the decompose skill was checked against Mendix's own documentation after it was drafted.
  • mxcli defects found along the way were drafted as an upstream report: 53 drafted, 3 later withdrawn, 47 ready to file. The staged files live in the separate personal-toolkit repo. Nothing has been filed yet.

Which model did what

Opus took the hard work: probes, slice builds, gate analysis, and writing the skills. Sonnet took recipe work: candidate tables, bug hunts, report drafting, shrinking bug repros. Fable ran one early extraction. The orchestrator coordinated the workers and merged their output.

2. How the app-to-MDL rebuild went

AppSizeResult
Banking (Mendix 11.13, same version as target)155 documents155 emitted, 154 identical (1 doc-comment drift). mx check 0 errors, build succeeded, structural diff empty. 35 s. Essentially lossless.
GenAI Showcase (Mendix 11.12)48 modules; 36 regenerated; 2,776 catalog documents29 of 36 modules landed. 579 identical, 271 drift, 1,993 failed to execute. After repair, mx check 14 errors, build FAILED.

The small app shows the idea works. The large app shows a long tail of defects in mxcli's own text output. Much of the loss was our extractor's fault, not the tool's: 7 whole modules (1,520 of the 1,993 failures) were silently dropped when our quarantine step could not map an error. That is the next fix.

The repair loop we built

  1. CheckValidate each module's script. The check is all-or-nothing per file.
  2. QuarantineMove the statements the check rejects into a deferred file, with the statements that depend on them.
  3. ExecuteRun the rest. Execution stops at the first statement it cannot write.
  4. ResumeDefer that statement with the exact error and keep going. Retry deferred ones in passes.

Why it exists: the first full run executed 1 of 36 modules, because one defect sank the other 35.

Done-criterion, confirmed by the user: "build-green with hand-offs". mx check 0 errors, build succeeded, and every document either landed or is a named hand-off with a reason. The hand-off count is the progress measure. The build decides, not describe-identity: one attribute sort was describe-identical and still failed the build.

Correction: we first wrote that message definitions cannot be expressed. They turned out to be expressible, so that item moved off the hand-off list.

6. Proven, not proven, and next

ProvenNot proven
Same-version single-module rehost is essentially lossless (154 of 155).A large rehost reaching build-green. GenAI stopped at 14 errors and a failed build.
